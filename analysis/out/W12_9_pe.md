# W12 idea 9: positional encodings

All numbers: hESC, hero settings (1-layer SAGE, `had` decoder, `--split batch`, no Geneformer), epoch chosen on validation AUROC, refit on all training rows. Reproduce: `python3.12 analysis/w12_9_report.py` (writes `analysis/out/w12_9_pe.txt`). Runs: `analysis/jobs/w12_9_screen.txt`, `analysis/jobs/w12_conf_a.txt`, `analysis/jobs/w12_ho_a.txt`.

## 1. Where the idea comes from

Laplacian eigenvector encodings and random-walk structural encodings (Dwivedi et al., 2020 and 2022) give each node a coordinate that says where it sits in the graph, because message passing alone cannot tell two structurally similar nodes apart. PEG (Wang et al., ICLR 2022) uses a distance between the encodings of the two endpoints, which suits link prediction better than concatenation because the distance does not depend on the arbitrary basis of the eigenvectors. Here the motivation was the finding that the hero knows gene popularity and local neighbourhood means but has no notion of which community of the graph a gene belongs to. A gene embedding that carries its community could help rank genes within a TF.

## 2. Method in short

Flags (all default off): `--pe {none,lap,rw}`, `--pe_dim k`, `--pe_graph {grn,coexp,ppi}`, `--pe_use {concat,dist,prod}`, `--pe_fold`, `--pe_flip`, `--pe_rand`, `--fold_batches`.

- `lap`: the k lowest non-trivial eigenvectors of the normalised Laplacian of the non-isolated nodes, scaled by sqrt(n); isolated nodes get zeros. Random sign flip per eigenvector per training batch.
- `rw`: diagonal of the first k powers of the random-walk matrix (RWSE), standardised per column. No sign ambiguity.
- `concat`: PE appended to the node features before the SAGE layer. `dist`/`prod` (PEG-style): ||pe_t - pe_g|| (one number) or pe_t * pe_g (k numbers) enter the decoder only.
- Graphs: `coexp` (label-free gene-gene kNN of the file, `coexp_graph`), `ppi` (label-free STRING), `grn` (the training TF->target graph).
- Leak handling for `grn`. A PE built from the whole training graph contains every training pair's own edge (the two endpoints end up close), while test edges are absent: the same mismatch that `--split batch` removes for message passing. Two versions were run. `--pe_fold 1` (called "fold-safe"): the training pairs are split into 5 folds, batches come from one fold, and the PE for that batch is computed on the training graph without the fold's edges (Laplacian eigenvectors are sign-aligned to the full-graph ones); evaluation uses the PE of the full training graph. `--pe_fold 0` (called "leak"): one PE from the full training graph for all batches. Because fold-pure batching itself changes training, a control with the same batching and no PE was run (`pe_foldctl`). The PE-specific own-edge diagnostic of `--own_edge_diag` was not extended to PEs (it only removes edges from the message-passing graph); the leak is measured by the leak versus fold-safe contrast.
- `coexp` and `ppi` graphs use no labels, so no pair is encoded through its own edge.
- Random control `--pe_rand 1`: fixed Gaussian vectors of the same size (acts as a node identity feature).
- Knobs tried at seed 42: k in {8, 16, 32} for both kinds on `coexp`; `ppi` and `grn` at k = 16; `dist` and `prod` at k = 16. Families to confirm (3 seeds) were picked by looking at the whole seed-42 table, test columns included, so the selection can only favour the PEs; no family is better than the hero, which makes the negative result stronger, not weaker.

## 4. Why

Measured:
- No PE improves within-TF over the hero. The PEs that come closest (lap on `ppi` or `coexp` k=32) are 0.002 to 0.003 below, inside the noise of 3 seeds.
- Random vectors of the same size cost 0.007, so unique per-gene vectors are mildly harmful (they give the model a way to memorise genes). The informative PEs are only better than random by 0.004 to 0.006 (lap ppi vs random: +0.0042, p = 0.035; lap coexp k=32: +0.0055, p = 0.17). The graphs carry a little real community information, but not enough to repay the cost of the extra input.
- The fold-safe GRN encodings are the worst variants (-0.019). Part of this is the fold-pure batching alone (-0.0066 with no PE). The rest (-0.0127 for lap, relative to the control) is a mismatch: the training PE comes from 80% of the edges, evaluation uses 100%. The "leak" versions are better than the fold-safe ones (+0.0126 lap, p = 0.04; +0.0111 rw, p = 0.02) while still below the hero. The own-edge leak therefore does not help the test score here.
- Where a gain could sit (genes far from any known target of the TF, or rarely targeted genes): strata of test pairs by maximum cosine to the TF's known targets and by gene popularity show differences of at most 0.005 AUROC for the best-by-validation family (`rw coexp k=16`), and no stratum with a gain (`w12_9_pe.txt`, section "Where the gain sits").
- Score/popularity Spearman is unchanged (0.731 hero, 0.738 rw coexp).

Guess (not tested): the first SAGE layer already averages expression over the TF's targets, so a gene's co-expression community is largely in the features; adding the community coordinates a second time brings noise (extra input dimensions with about 3000 positive training edges) rather than new information.

## 5. Verdict

Regressed or neutral everywhere; not worth tuning. If revisited: PE on `ppi` with a low-dimensional `dist` feature only, since those were the only variants at or above the hero in the one-seed screen, and the evidence for them is within noise.
